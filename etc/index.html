The ETC model · a benchmark that needs no training

Run ETC first.It scores the data, not your model.

ETC is a formula, independent of data — no training, no dataset, no scenario baked in. It captures one thing: how much the motion already tells you about wake. Before crediting the architecture, it's worth knowing what a formula already gets.

scroll

First, the metric: AUROC.

Every score here is an AUROC — the area under the ROC curve. In plain terms: the model gives every minute a probability of being sleep, and AUROC is the chance that a random true-sleep minute gets a higher probability than a random true-wake minute. 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect.

ETC draws the floor. The shaded region beneath its curve is the separation you get with no model at all — just smoothed motion. Real models earn the gold band above it; a curve that drops below ETC has been beaten by a formula that needs no data, training, or compute.

A high AUROC looks like a strong model. Often it's mostly an easy dataset.

A single AUROC is usually read as a measure of the model. It actually bundles two things together: how separable the data already was, and how much the model added on top of that.

ETC is that floor made concrete. It blurs the motion signal — the same Gaussian smoothing an image editor uses to soften a photo — and reads the blurred motion straight off as a probability of being awake. That's the entire model: movement means wake. Run it on any dataset and the score it returns is free to every model.

01 / four headlines

Four datasets, four reported AUROCs.

Read top to bottom, the model looks strong everywhere and best on SEN‑A. These are the numbers a paper would put in the abstract.

02 / the floor

On SEN‑A, ETC alone reaches 0.93.

The model's 0.95 sits just above it. The gold band — the part the model actually added — is only 0.018 wide.

03 / the harder set

SURF_Apple's headline is lowest, at 0.73 — but ETC reaches only 0.64.

So the model added 0.091 here, roughly five times its contribution on SEN‑A. The lower headline came from harder data, not a weaker model.

04 / almost all data

On Somerset, ETC reaches 0.784 and the model 0.786.

The gold band nearly disappears. Almost the entire score is the data; the model added ~0.003.

05 / two orderings

Same four numbers. Two different rankings.

By headline AUROC, SEN‑A leads and SURF_Apple trails. By skill above ETC, that order reverses. Reporting ETC alongside the headline is what tells them apart.

AUROC is read as a measure of the model. Most of it is a measure of the data.

AUROC  =  ETC (how easy the data is)  +  marginal gains (yours)
