The ETC blur · tuning by timescale

Not one number.A bank of gauges.

ETC blurs the motion signal and reads the result as a probability of being awake. That blur has a width — and changing it asks the same question at different timescales: does motion predict wake second to second, or minute to minute? Each width is its own gauge, and reading the whole bank tells you where a dataset's signal actually lives.

What matters is the effective width — in minutes.

A narrow blur is a local gauge: it tracks brief bursts of motion, sharp but jittery. A wide blur is a global gauge: steady, but it smears short wake away. Sweep the width and you read the same night at every timescale at once.

The bank has a sweet spot — and its shape is a fingerprint.

Toggle the wake character and watch the sweep. For sustained wake the readout stays flat: the signal survives any amount of smoothing. For transient wake it peaks near the event timescale and falls off as a wide blur smears the bursts away. That shape — not a single number — tells you where a dataset's separability lives. It's why Somerset, with its briefer wake, is the dataset most sensitive to σ.

One number tells you how good a gauge is. The bank tells you what the data is made of.
