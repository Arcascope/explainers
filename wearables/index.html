Wearable Validation Atlas

Values are as reported in each paper. Bias is device minus PSG throughout (signs flipped for Stucky 2021, Concheiro-Moscoso 2023 and Svensson 2024, whose tables use PSG minus device; two of Concheiro-Moscoso's three bias CIs had a sign typo on one bound and are shown corrected). Sensitivity is for sleep and specificity for wake; Stucky 2021, Lee 2023 and Labie 2025 report wake as the positive class, so their two values are swapped here. F1 is shown for the sleep class. Ravindran 2023 reports sleep-class F1 directly, so those four marks are drawn solid; Lee 2023 reports wake-class F1, from which the sleep-class value follows exactly given sensitivity and specificity; Chinoy 2021, Concheiro-Moscoso 2023 and Mouritzen 2020 publish pooled epoch counts, so their sleep F1 is exact; for every other study it is estimated from reported sensitivity, specificity and the PSG sleep prevalence in the analysis window (hollow marks throughout; the tooltip states the basis). Miller 2020's sleep periods were set manually by the researchers rather than detected by the device. Where a paper reports only the SD of TST differences, limits of agreement are derived as bias ± 1.96·SD and drawn hollow; Miller 2022 and Kainec 2024 report proportional limits instead, and Chinoy 2021 limits are ±2 SD as published. For accuracy, sensitivity, specificity and κ a good zone can also require a narrow confidence interval; that half of the rule is evaluated only where the paper reports a CI. F1 and PABAK have no published CIs, so their zones are plain thresholds. Diamonds are manufacturer white papers, not peer-reviewed studies: Apple (updated October 2025) reports only medians of per-night sensitivity and specificity for its 2022 and 2025 sleep-stages algorithms, on a 166-participant validation set and a separate, independent clinical PSG cohort, with no bias, limits of agreement or two-stage agreement statistics; those dots are labelled "(median)" and are not directly comparable with the means or pooled-epoch values elsewhere (the central_statistic column and the table's Stat column record mean, median, pooled, mixed or unclear for every row). Google Research (Google 2026, Charton et al.) reports per-night mean sensitivity and specificity and a TST Bland-Altman plot for the 2026 Fitbit and Pixel Watch algorithm on 50 PSG nights. Both papers give only four-stage kappa, which is not shown here. Some dots carry a short Note in the tooltip flagging something about how that row's numbers were produced - the reference standard, the analysis window, how the device data were obtained, how much of it was captured, or the status of the algorithm. Each row reflects the device and algorithm as they were at the time that study collected its data, which for the older rows predates later firmware and algorithm updates; where a paper names the algorithm version it is given in the device line of the tooltip. A study contributes one dot per device it tested, so a multi-device study contributes many: Lee 2023 alone accounts for 11 of them. The counters above report papers, dots and devices separately. Where a study reports the same device under more than one condition - a different night, hand, scoring algorithm, protocol or sleep-period definition - only its primary analysis is plotted. The variants are kept in the dataset and marked condition in the row_type column. Full dataset with source locations and caveats: wearable_validation_metrics.csv. Static figure: PNG, PDF.
